MEDICAL GLOSSARY — CLINICAL TERMS AND ABBREVIATIONS
Version 1.8 | Updated 2026 | Internal Reference Document

==============================================================
SECTION 1: CARDIOVASCULAR TERMS
==============================================================

Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS): Umbrella term for myocardial infarction (STEMI or NSTEMI) and unstable angina, all caused by rupture or erosion of an atherosclerotic plaque.

Angina Pectoris: Chest pain or discomfort caused by myocardial ischemia (inadequate oxygen supply to heart muscle). Stable angina occurs predictably with exertion; unstable angina (UA) occurs at rest or with minimal exertion.

Arrhythmia: Abnormal heart rate or rhythm. Includes tachyarrhythmias (fast) and bradyarrhythmias (slow). Examples: atrial fibrillation (AF), ventricular tachycardia (VT), heart block.

Atrial Fibrillation (AF/AFib): Irregular and often rapid atrial electrical activity causing disorganized atrial contractions. Leading cause of cardioembolic stroke. Classified as paroxysmal (<7 days), persistent (>7 days), long-standing persistent, or permanent.

Cardiac Output (CO): Volume of blood pumped by the heart per minute. CO = Stroke Volume (SV) × Heart Rate (HR). Normal: 4-8 L/min.

Cardiomyopathy: Disease of the heart muscle. Types include dilated (DCM), hypertrophic (HCM), restrictive, and arrhythmogenic right ventricular (ARVC).

Coronary Artery Disease (CAD): Atherosclerotic narrowing of coronary arteries leading to myocardial ischemia. Major risk factor for MI.

Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT): Blood clot in a deep vein, usually the legs. Can embolise to pulmonary vasculature causing pulmonary embolism (PE).

Ejection Fraction (EF): Percentage of blood pumped out of the left ventricle with each contraction. Normal LVEF: 55-70%. HFrEF: EF < 40%; HFpEF: EF >= 50%.

Heart Failure (HF): Condition in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs. Classified by EF: HFrEF (<40%), HFmrEF (40-49%), HFpEF (>=50%).

Hypertension (HTN): Sustained elevation of systolic BP >= 130 mmHg or diastolic BP >= 80 mmHg. "Silent killer" — often asymptomatic until organ damage occurs.

Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH): Thickening of left ventricular wall due to pressure overload (e.g., hypertension). Associated with increased risk of arrhythmias and cardiac events.

Myocardial Infarction (MI): Heart attack — irreversible necrosis of myocardial tissue due to prolonged ischemia. STEMI: ST-elevation MI (complete coronary occlusion). NSTEMI: non-ST-elevation MI (partial occlusion).

Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI): Minimally invasive procedure to open narrowed coronary arteries, usually with a balloon (PTCA) and/or stent placement.

Pulmonary Embolism (PE): Obstruction of pulmonary vasculature by a thrombus (usually from DVT), fat, air, or tumor. Presents with dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, tachycardia, and hypoxia.

Stroke Volume (SV): Volume of blood ejected by the left ventricle per beat. Determined by preload, afterload, and contractility.

SECTION 2: ENDOCRINE AND METABOLIC TERMS
==============================================================

Body Mass Index (BMI): Weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared (kg/m²). Classification: Underweight <18.5, Normal 18.5-24.9, Overweight 25-29.9, Obese I 30-34.9, Obese II 35-39.9, Obese III >=40.

Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA): Life-threatening complication of diabetes (primarily T1DM) characterised by hyperglycemia (>250 mg/dL), ketonemia/ketonuria, and metabolic acidosis (pH <7.3, bicarbonate <18 mEq/L).

Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c): Measures average blood glucose over the preceding 2-3 months by assessing the percentage of hemoglobin with glucose attached. Gold standard for long-term glycemic control assessment.

Hyperglycemia: Blood glucose above normal. Fasting glucose >100 mg/dL. Symptoms of overt hyperglycemia (polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, weight loss) typically appear at glucose >200-250 mg/dL.

Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS): Severe complication of T2DM with extreme hyperglycemia (>600 mg/dL), severe dehydration, hyperosmolality, and altered consciousness. No significant ketosis distinguishes from DKA.

Hypoglycemia: Blood glucose below normal. ADA defines hypoglycemia alert value as <70 mg/dL; clinically significant as <54 mg/dL; severe if requiring assistance.

Insulin Resistance: Impaired biological response to insulin in insulin-sensitive tissues (liver, skeletal muscle, adipose tissue). Central feature of T2DM, metabolic syndrome, and PCOS.

Metabolic Syndrome (MetS): Cluster of conditions increasing cardiovascular and T2DM risk. Requires >=3 of: waist circumference >102 cm (men) or >88 cm (women); triglycerides >=150 mg/dL; HDL <40 (men) or <50 (women) mg/dL; BP >=130/85 mmHg; fasting glucose >=100 mg/dL.

Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH): Pituitary hormone that regulates thyroid function. Elevated TSH indicates hypothyroidism; suppressed TSH indicates hyperthyroidism.

SECTION 3: RENAL TERMS
==============================================================

Acute Kidney Injury (AKI): Abrupt decline in kidney function. KDIGO definition: rise in serum creatinine >=0.3 mg/dL within 48h, OR rise to >=1.5× baseline within 7 days, OR urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for >6h.

Albuminuria: Excessive albumin in urine. UACR <30 mg/g normal; 30-300 moderately increased; >300 severely increased (formerly "macroalbuminuria"). Marker of glomerular injury and cardiovascular risk.

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD): Abnormalities of kidney structure or function lasting >3 months. Staged by eGFR: G1 >=90, G2 60-89, G3a 45-59, G3b 30-44, G4 15-29, G5 <15 (kidney failure).

Creatinine: Waste product from muscle metabolism filtered by kidneys. Serum creatinine rises when GFR falls. Reference ranges: Men 0.74-1.35 mg/dL; Women 0.59-1.04 mg/dL.

Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR): Calculated measure of kidney filtration capacity using CKD-EPI or MDRD equation. Normal: >90 mL/min/1.73 m². The best overall index of kidney function.

End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD): CKD stage G5 — kidney failure requiring renal replacement therapy (dialysis or transplant) for survival.

Glomerulonephritis (GN): Inflammation of the glomeruli. Can be primary (IgA nephropathy, FSGS, membranous) or secondary (lupus nephritis, diabetic nephropathy).

Nephrotic Syndrome: Massive proteinuria (>3.5 g/day), hypoalbuminemia, edema, hyperlipidemia. Caused by podocyte damage (FSGS, minimal change disease, membranous).

Uremia: Toxic syndrome caused by accumulation of waste products in kidney failure. Symptoms: fatigue, nausea, cognitive impairment, pruritus, pericarditis, encephalopathy.

SECTION 4: RESPIRATORY TERMS
==============================================================

Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS): Life-threatening inflammatory lung injury characterised by diffuse alveolar damage, bilateral pulmonary infiltrates, and refractory hypoxemia (PaO2/FiO2 ratio <300 mmHg). Berlin definition: mild <300, moderate <200, severe <100.

Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD): Persistent, progressive airflow limitation caused by airway and alveolar abnormalities from significant exposure to noxious particles or gases (primarily cigarette smoke). FEV1/FVC ratio <0.70 post-bronchodilator confirms diagnosis.

FEV1 (Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 second): Volume of air forcibly exhaled in the first second of a maximally forced expiration. Key spirometric measure in COPD and asthma. Expressed as percentage of predicted.

FVC (Forced Vital Capacity): Total volume of air forcibly exhaled after maximal inspiration. FEV1/FVC ratio distinguishes obstructive (reduced ratio) from restrictive (normal or elevated ratio) disease.

Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA): Recurrent episodes of complete (apnea) or partial (hypopnea) upper airway obstruction during sleep, causing oxygen desaturation and sleep fragmentation. Diagnosed by polysomnography with AHI >=5 events/hour.

Oxygen Saturation (SpO2): Non-invasive measure of hemoglobin oxygen saturation by pulse oximetry. Normal: 95-100%. Action threshold: 88-92% in COPD patients; maintain >94% in most other patients.

Pulmonary Function Tests (PFTs): Spirometry measures lung volumes and airflow. Includes FEV1, FVC, FEV1/FVC, TLC, RV, DLCO. Essential for diagnosing and staging lung disease.

==============================================================
SECTION 5: NEUROLOGICAL TERMS
==============================================================

Cerebrovascular Accident (CVA): Stroke — sudden neurological deficits caused by disruption of cerebral blood supply. Ischemic stroke (85%): thrombotic or embolic. Hemorrhagic stroke (15%): intracerebral or subarachnoid hemorrhage.

Intracranial Pressure (ICP): Pressure within the skull. Normal: 7-15 mmHg. Elevated ICP (>20 mmHg) causes brain herniation. Managed with head positioning, osmotherapy (mannitol, hypertonic saline), sedation, surgical decompression.

Multiple Sclerosis (MS): Autoimmune demyelinating disease of the CNS. Relapsing-remitting (RRMS) most common form. Diagnose via McDonald criteria (MRI + clinical criteria). Treatment: disease-modifying therapies.

Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA): Transient neurological dysfunction from focal brain ischemia without infarction; typically <24 hours, usually <1 hour. High risk of subsequent stroke (ABCD2 score used for risk stratification).

SECTION 6: LABORATORY ABBREVIATIONS AND REFERENCE RANGES
==============================================================

Complete Blood Count (CBC):
- WBC: 4,500-11,000 cells/µL
- RBC: 4.5-5.5 million cells/µL (men); 4.0-5.0 (women)
- Hemoglobin (Hgb/Hb): 13.5-17.5 g/dL (men); 12.0-15.5 g/dL (women)
- Hematocrit (Hct): 41-53% (men); 36-46% (women)
- MCV: 80-100 fL
- Platelets: 150,000-400,000/µL

Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP) / Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP):
- Sodium (Na): 136-145 mEq/L
- Potassium (K): 3.5-5.1 mEq/L
- Chloride (Cl): 98-107 mEq/L
- Bicarbonate (HCO3): 22-29 mEq/L
- BUN (Blood Urea Nitrogen): 7-25 mg/dL
- Creatinine: 0.74-1.35 mg/dL (men); 0.59-1.04 mg/dL (women)
- Glucose: 70-99 mg/dL (fasting)
- Calcium: 8.5-10.5 mg/dL
- Albumin: 3.5-5.0 g/dL
- AST: 10-40 U/L
- ALT: 7-56 U/L
- Alkaline Phosphatase: 44-147 U/L
- Total Bilirubin: 0.1-1.2 mg/dL

Lipid Panel:
- Total Cholesterol: Desirable <200 mg/dL
- LDL: Optimal <100 mg/dL; Very High Risk target <70 mg/dL; Extreme risk target <55 mg/dL
- HDL: Low risk M >=40 mg/dL, F >=50 mg/dL; Optimal >=60 mg/dL
- Triglycerides: Normal <150 mg/dL; High 200-499; Very high >=500 mg/dL

Coagulation:
- PT (Prothrombin Time): 11-13 seconds
- INR: 0.9-1.1 (normal); 2-3 (therapeutic for AF/VTE); 2.5-3.5 (mechanical heart valve)
- PTT (Partial Thromboplastin Time): 25-35 seconds; therapeutic (heparin): 60-100 seconds

Thyroid:
- TSH: 0.4-4.0 mIU/L
- Free T4: 0.8-1.8 ng/dL

Cardiac Biomarkers:
- Troponin I (conventional): <0.04 ng/mL
- High-sensitivity troponin T: <14 ng/L (99th percentile; varies by assay)
- BNP: <100 pg/mL; NT-proBNP: <125 pg/mL (<75 y/o); <450 pg/mL (>75 y/o)
- CK-MB: <5% of total CK; CK-MB >6% suggests myocardial origin

Inflammatory Markers:
- CRP (C-reactive protein): <1.0 mg/L low risk; 1-3 moderate; >3 high (for CV risk)
- ESR: 0-20 mm/h (men); 0-30 mm/h (women)
- Ferritin: 20-250 ng/mL (men); 10-120 ng/mL (women)
- Procalcitonin (PCT): <0.05 ng/mL normal; >0.5 bacterial sepsis likely; >2 severe sepsis
